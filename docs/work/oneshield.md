## The challenge

Insurance platforms are configured, not just installed. Much of OneShield's product behavior is driven by metadata and schemas, so customers and internal teams needed documentation that explained both the applications and the structures underneath them, while each release brought new notes, advisories, and training needs.

## What I delivered

- **User manuals** for the metadata applications, published on Confluence and SharePoint and in Word
- **Schema documentation** for JSON metadata and XML schemas, tested against the product
- **Release communication**: release notes, advisories, and executive summaries for each release
- **Documentation for in-house AI software**
- **CI/CD documentation** for the build and release toolchain: Jenkins, Bamboo, JFrog, and Nexus
- **Video training modules** built with Camtasia and delivered through the LMS

## How I led

- **Team leadership.** I led the technical writing team, reviewed every writer's work, and mentored junior writers.
- **Workflow.** I streamlined documentation tasks in Azure DevOps so documentation work was planned alongside engineering work.
- **Collaboration.** I worked with core engineering, QA, SMEs, product managers, UI designers, and UAT teams on business process flows and product behavior.
- **Knowledge sharing.** I ran knowledge transfer sessions on the areas I owned.

## What this shows

- Leading a documentation team inside an engineering organization
- Documenting data structures and pipelines, not only user interfaces
- Combining written and video training for different learning needs
